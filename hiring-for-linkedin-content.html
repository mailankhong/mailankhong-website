Hiring for LinkedIn content: what to look for in a hire, an agency or your own team

Mai-Lan Khong, content engineer. Published 7 October 2026

When you hire for LinkedIn content, I think the label matters less than the work. A content lead on your payroll, an agency, or your own team with training can each do the work, and each can miss it. So I would look at four things first: who gets the founder talking, who holds the point of view, who has the judgment to cut, and who builds the system.

This page is for the founder or the marketing lead of a B2B company deciding who should run their LinkedIn content. It does not tell you which of the three to pick. It tells you what I would look for in whichever you pick. Where I stand: I am Content Lead at Frontal, where I build and run the content machine behind a founder's LinkedIn, or train a team to run its own. Frontal offers both as services, so I am not a neutral voice on two of the three options.

What should I look for when I hire someone to lead my LinkedIn content?

Look for a person who can write and also think in systems. This is my own view from running content, not a tested checklist.

On this page, a content lead means the person who owns your LinkedIn content: what is said, in whose voice, and where it goes. That is the only part of the role this page covers.

In my post of 2 Jun 2026 I passed on what a founder had sent me: "So hard to find someone who's good at content AND can think in systems." In that post's words, "He'd already burned through a couple of writers." The person missing from the market, as I put it there, is "the writer who also thinks like a growth operator".

The four things above are my short list for the work, whoever does it. For the person, my longer list is in that post of 2 Jun 2026, which I wrote for content people. It has five points: three are the same as above (point of view, system, judgment) and two more are about the person (reading the buyer, owning distribution). Read from the hiring side:

  1. They can hold a point of view in someone else's voice. "Holding a real opinion in their name, across 100 posts, without sliding back into the AI default or leaking your own voice in".
  2. They read the buyer before the trends. "The topic is downstream of the ICP, not the other way around." (ICP: the buyer you want to reach.)
  3. They own distribution. "You treat distribution as your job, not the algorithm's problem."
  4. They build the system. "You could leave a whole content operating system behind that runs without you in the room."
  5. They have judgment about the work. "This is taste, and it's the one thing that doesn't get cheaper as the tools get better."

LinkedIn content agency or in-house content lead?

Either can work, and I would not start from the label. On this page, in-house means someone on your payroll, and an agency means an outside team or person you pay to do the work. What I would compare is the work each one will really do.

In my post of 19 May 2026 I wrote: "When AI floods a market, the mediocre middle collapses while excellence becomes more valuable, not less." Two of the examples I gave in that post of 19 May 2026 sit on the two sides of this choice, one inside a company and one outside it:

  • "The in-house junior writing on a template."
  • "The founder who outsources "competent posts" to a freelancer."

That post is my opinion about where the market is going. It is not a study, and it does not say that one side is better.

The four things I would ask of a hire and of an agency

  • Who gets the founder talking? When posts go out under a founder's name, someone has to get real material out of that founder, again and again.
  • Who holds the point of view? Someone has to keep one opinion steady across months of posts.
  • Who has the judgment to cut? In my post of 19 May 2026 I counted "the editorial judgment to cut 60% of a draft" among the work that is worth more now.
  • Who builds the system? Someone has to build it so that it still runs when they are not in the room.

What I called worth more now, in the same post of 19 May 2026: "discovery work, voice depth, and editorial taste, the parts of the value chain AI can't shortcut".

What this page does not cover

Cost. I keep prices off this site, so this page will not help you with the budget side of the choice.

Can I hire someone to do my LinkedIn?

Yes. What you cannot hand over is the founder's own material: their experience, their observations, their voice. From my post of 24 Mar 2026: "AI is only as good as what you feed into it. Your experience. Your specific observations. The voice that exists before you open a prompt." I see the gap between a founder who brings something real and one who doesn't. In the words of that post of 24 Mar 2026: "The output difference is humbling."

In the program I run, that material comes out in a conversation. From my post of 6 Aug 2026: "Voice capture happens in a real conversation." So I would ask a hire or an agency how they would capture the founder's voice, and how often they would talk.

Or train the team you already have

The third option is to keep the work inside and train the people you have to run it. The same four things apply: after the training, your own team has to get the founder talking, hold the point of view, cut, and run the system.
