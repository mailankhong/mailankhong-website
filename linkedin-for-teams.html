LinkedIn employee advocacy: how to get your team posting

Mai-Lan Khong, content engineer. Published 30 September 2026

To get executives and employees posting, I make it easy and make it theirs: their voice captured in a conversation, the followers and the messages on their own profile, and a plan for what happens to each post. That is the setup of the program I run at Frontal (section 5), and the one I train teams to run.

This page is for the founder or the marketing lead who has to get their people posting, and decide how the team runs it: the setup, the founder's part, what to post, and how to train the team.

What is employee advocacy?

Employee advocacy is when a company's people talk about their work and their company on their own social profiles, to their own networks. Sprout Social describes it as the moment "When employees share company content, promote brand messages and engage in industry conversations through their personal networks".

On LinkedIn, I run the version where people publish their own posts, about their own work, on their own profiles. Resharing the company's posts is the smaller part. From my post of 28 Aug 2026, this is the layer of your presence built by "Reposts, tags, recommendations, employee advocacy, people sharing your ideas", and "Your reputation gets much stronger when other people start carrying it for you."

How do you get executives to actually post on LinkedIn?

In the program I run, I make it easy and make it theirs: their voice captured in a conversation, the followers and the messages belonging to them, and a plan for what happens to each post after it goes out. The operating setup, from my post of 6 Aug 2026:

  1. Voice first, in a conversation. "Voice capture happens in a real conversation." A form asking for their tone gets a guess, not their voice.
  2. It belongs to them. "The followers and the DMs belong to the employee. If they belong to the company page instead, nobody stays motivated past week 6."
  3. Distribution is planned. "And distribution is planned: who engages in the first hour, where each post gets reshared, and which posts turned into booked calls." And: "A screenshot of likes pinned in Slack doesn't count as measurement."

Executives have a job in the program that nobody else can do. From the same post: "Founders and execs open and close the story. They give the company its point of view, and they're the names buyers want to trust before handing over money."

What executives and founders say first

Two answers often come up early: "I don't really have a following" and "I don't want to be an influencer".

  • "I don't really have a following." It matters less than it used to. From my post of 8 Jun 2026, "these days the algorithm matches you to people by topic, not by your follower count." And from my post of 16 Sep 2026: "Follower count is not the gate."
  • "I don't want to be an influencer" The program doesn't ask them to be one. Its job is that the right buyers know who they are when the need comes. From my post of 28 Aug 2026, the goal is to "Build enough association, recency, social proof and trust that when the right problem appears, people think of you." Reach is a side effect: "Reach comes anyway when the 3 layers run, despite it was not the goal." (my post, 6 Aug 2026).

Is it worth posting from the company page rather than people?

Run the program on people, and keep the company page in good shape too. People carry the trust. From my post of 7 Sep 2026: "The personal post still matters most. It travels with your name, your title, your company and your years of experience, and that is what AI checks before it trusts a source."

The company page still has a job: it is one of the places AI answers read about your company. My advice in my post of 16 Sep 2026: "Fix the company page anyway". And when there is paid budget, it can go behind people's posts that already won, "instead of random boosts on the corporate page" (my post, 6 Aug 2026).

Does posting from people's own profiles drive revenue?

It can. The program I run answers to pipeline, not to engagement, and I can't promise revenue for your company. What changes inside the team when it works, from my post of 6 Aug 2026:

  • "It's a sales team that believes what they sell, because they see themselves in the content."
  • "It's deals closing faster because the buyer spent two months reading the seller."

What is founder-led marketing?

Founder-led marketing is when the founder's own voice is the company's main way to reach buyers: their posts, their opinions, their name. The B2B Playbook sums it up in one line: "Founder-led is not a content strategy. It's a trust strategy."

In a team program, the founder is one voice of three, and an important one. From my post of 6 Aug 2026: "If only the founder posts, the whole thing smells like a brand exercise. If only the operators post, the company has no point of view, just employees with opinions."

What is EGC vs UGC?

EGC is employee-generated content: content people make "in their own capacity, this time as employees". UGC is user-generated content: "reviews, photos, videos, or any content created by customers on social media platforms". (Both definitions: Oktopost.)

In the three voices of a team program, the operators make the EGC. When customers post about you, that is UGC. From my post of 6 Aug 2026:

  • "Operators sit in the middle: the AEs, the engineers, the RevOps people, talking about the work itself, with proof and real opinions."
  • "Customers and the wider network bring the one thing nobody inside the company can fake: the testimonial and the warm intro."

Training a team to run it

Teach the judgment before the tools. A team that only learns which buttons to press can't tell when the posts drift.

What that looks like in a session, from the same post: "Write the hook yourself. Explain why this CTA fits this audience. Tell me what's wrong with this draft before the AI touches it."

The request I hear once the system works is the one to resist. From the same post: "Do we even need more training? Just show us which buttons to press." My answer there: "It's slower. But a system you can't judge is a system that owns you."

How many LinkedIn posts a week should a B2B SaaS founder publish?

My own rule is 3 to 5 posts a week, from the 100 LinkedIn rules I published on 1 May 2026. Pick a number you can keep for months, not one you can keep for a week.

What you post has to add up, too. From my post of 1 Sep 2026: "Output is a schedule. Coherence is a shape."

And don't copy someone else's number. From my post of 26 May 2026: "When advice is built on someone's own account, it won't promise a successful transfer to yours."

How do I choose content pillars for a founder's LinkedIn account?

Pick 5 or 6 themes the founder already holds a position on, in their own words, so every post traces back to one of them. Then write down what they will not post about.

From my list of rules of 1 May 2026: "A no-go list beats a topic list."

The pillars are how people come to recognise a founder. From my post of 1 Sep 2026: "Your expertise gets easier to describe with every post, and your voice makes the whole pattern feel like it belongs to one person."

Why does my LinkedIn content get no engagement even though it is useful?

In my experience, because useful is not the same as something people want to react to in public, and because the post is only part of the result.

From the same post: "You're not writing something to be read. You're writing something people want to be seen standing next to."

The other part is everything around the post. From my post of 10 Jun 2026: "I used to think a post lived or died on how good it was. I was wrong." And: "The post is maybe half the result at its best." The rest is showing up in the right conversations and turning readers into conversations of your own.
