My AI search test log

Mai-Lan Khong, content engineer. Published 5 October 2026

In September 2026 I rebuilt this site to test one thing: whether an AI assistant, asked about the topics I work on without my name in the question, ever points to me. This page is the log of that test: what kinds of questions I ask, how I count, the numbers from each full run, and what I changed on the site in between.

In the first run, on 28 September, before my new homepage and topic pages, the answer was no. Asked 25 questions without my name, three times each, neither engine named me once.

What I am testing

From my post of 16 Sep 2026: "There is a version of your company that exists only inside ChatGPT. It was assembled from whatever you left in public, and it answers questions about you when you are not in the room."

I write about this on LinkedIn, so I test it on my own site, in the open. The site was rebuilt around three topics, each with its own page: AI content systems, content to drive B2B pipeline and LinkedIn programs for teams. Of the 25 test questions without my name, 16 are answered by a section of those pages. The other 9 are asked to see whether my name comes up where no page of mine answers. All of them were asked before the pages existed, and are asked again after Google has them.

How the test works

The test asks 27 questions, 3 times each, to two engines:

  • OpenAI with web search: OpenAI's model with web search, asked through OpenAI's API. It is the closest stand-in I have for ChatGPT. The ChatGPT app itself can answer differently.
  • Google's AI Overviews: the AI answer at the top of Google's results page, read as a searcher in the US, in English.

For each answer I count three things: does it name me (Mai-Lan Khong, in any of its spellings), does it cite a page of this site, does it cite my LinkedIn profile or one of my posts. A citation, in my own words from 9 Sep 2026: "A citation is the moment an AI answer links your post as its source. It is not a mention of your name and it is not a view."

Frontal, the company I work with, is counted apart and never as a point for me. When Google shows no AI answer for a question, that answer is left out, not counted as a miss. The main number is the 25 questions without my name. The two questions that carry a name in them are reported on their own.

The same question can get a different answer each time it is asked. In the first run, one of Google's three answers to one question cited my LinkedIn and the other two did not. So my rule, set before any run after it: one answer out of three moving on one question is not counted as a change. A change is read on the counts: for each engine and each of the three things I count, the share of answers received, on the 25 questions without my name together, and on each named question on its own. A count has changed when it is above its 28 September share in two full runs in a row, or below it in both. One run alone never shows a change, and a change first seen in December stays unconfirmed until a later run. Runs are compared only when they use the same questions, engines, model versions, number of answers, and Google's country and language. If one of those changes, a new series starts, and this page says so and shows no trend across it.

What I ask

27 questions, in five groups. I publish them word for word after the December run, not before: a page with my name next to each exact question could get me named for them, and then the test would partly be measuring this page. Keeping them off makes that less likely, it does not rule it out. This page is one of the changes (section 6), and any answer that cites it is reported on its own.

  • AI content systems (9 questions, 6 answered on that page): among them, building a content system with AI for a founder, keeping the founder's voice, the documents a content team needs, and scaling to several founders.
  • Content to drive B2B pipeline (6 questions, 5 answered on that page): among them, whether content brings pipeline and how to measure it, attribution, being cited in AI answers, and being ready for AI search.
  • LinkedIn programs for teams (8 questions, 5 answered on that page): among them, how often to post, getting executives to post, engagement, content pillars, and whether personal content brings revenue.
  • Who to hire or follow (2 questions, with no page of their own).
  • With a name in the question (2 questions, reported apart): "Who is Mai-Lan Khong?" and one question about content engineering at Frontal.

The themes of the other 7 questions no page answers wait for December, with the questions themselves.

21 of them come from an earlier test of 7 Sep 2026. The other 6 were added on 28 Sep, kept close to words used by buyers and by people who sell to them.

Before: 28 September 2026

The first run was on the evening of 28 September. It came before the new homepage, About page and topic pages, but not before everything: the first technical fixes to the site went live one minute before it began. Google's copy of my homepage was then still from 21 September. So it is the before picture for the changes of 29 and 30 September, and nearly, not provably, for the fixes of 28 September. All 162 answers came back, and every Google question had an AI answer.

What I changed since

  • 28 Sep: the site's technical fixes. One address per page, the text at the top of each page visible from the start, images from 29.5 MB down to 4.6 MB. The old meditation pages came down.
  • 29 Sep: a new homepage and About page, with my one line on both. My LinkedIn company page rewritten with the same line, and my LinkedIn profile updated.
  • 30 Sep: the three topic pages went live at 22:07. Google had all three in its index the same evening.
  • The day this page goes live: listed here too, because a page about the test is also something an engine can read.

Several changes landed in the same days, so if a number moves, this test cannot say which change did it.

What happens next

The full test runs again on the first Monday after the three topic pages are in Google's index, planned for Monday 5 October 2026, less than five days after they went live, and once more on Monday 14 December 2026, about ten weeks after. Engines read new pages on their own schedule, so 5 October is a first look, and December gives them more time to read the pages. Neither run can show that a page caused a change. Each run is added here with its date, under the same rules, next to the one before.

Every Monday until 14 December I also ask one question, "Who is Mai-Lan Khong?", to see how the engines describe me. I read it each week and do not publish it each week: 3 answers per engine a week are too few to read on their own.

What this test cannot tell

It asks two engines. Perplexity, Claude and Copilot are not in it. A mention or a citation is not a visit, and a visit is not a client. Even the visit is hard to trace, because, from my post of 14 Sep 2026, "a visit from an AI answer carries no record of which post it quoted". And it is one site and one person: what happens here is what happened here, not a promise for anyone else's site.
